## Why R3 was reopened

The original v1.8 R2 freeze was followed by a production-soak hold. The hold required at least one full five-lane v1.7 production day plus real-candidate review. By 2026-08-25, v1.7 had accumulated multiple full production days and exposed concrete knowledge gaps that were not hypothetical at freeze time.

The most visible symptom was WAIT-candidate drift toward extreme longshots and thin/player-prop tails where the apparent residual was often driven by one sportsbook being much longer than another rather than by an independently supported handicap. That production evidence justifies a focused R3 gap-closure pass without reopening broad discovery.

## Gap inventory

### P0 — extreme longshot / outlier interpretation

**Observed production problem:** extreme +400 to +1200 candidates were increasingly promoted to WAIT because a best-book quote sat above a two-book no-vig benchmark.

**v1.7/v1.8-R2 state:** favorite-longshot and bookmaker-structure work existed, but the library did not retrieve a sufficiently direct modern fixed-odds caution for extreme tails.

**R3 closure:** add Whelan (2024) directly. The paper documents worsening returns for longshots, especially extreme longshots, in 56,004 professional tennis matches and provides a competitive fixed-odds mechanism based on bettor disagreement and bookmaker risk aversion. Use as cross-sport mechanism/caution only; do not transport its numerical tennis calibration to other sports.

### P0 — low-liquidity soccer / book-dispersion interpretation

**Observed production problem:** obscure soccer and derivative tails could look attractive when one supported book was far longer than the other.

**v1.7/v1.8-R2 state:** strong general soccer efficiency and a 1X2-vs-Asian-handicap structure item existed, but liquidity-specific interpretation remained thin.

**R3 closure:** add Flepp, Nüesch & Franck (2017), based on more than 1.8 million bookmaker/exchange odds across 17,410 soccer matches. Relative quote quality changes with exchange liquidity. A high bookmaker quote in a thin market is therefore not, by itself, proof of fundamental mispricing.

### P0 — college football direct coverage

**Observed knowledge gap:** v1.7 had very sparse college-football coverage compared with NFL/MLB.

**R3 closure:** add Arscott (2023) on team-total/point-spread censoring bias and Paul & Weinbach (2009) on sportsbook behavior using actual NCAA football betting percentages. Preserve era/implementation caution; neither item authorizes mechanical betting.

### P0 — WNBA game-market coverage

**Observed knowledge gap:** v1.8 shadow correctly returned NR on WNBA moneylines because the candidate work was prop-specific.

**R3 closure:** add Paul & Weinbach (2014), a direct WNBA point-spread efficiency study using 2007–2012 data. It found public favorite/road-favorite preferences but no statistically significant profit from simple fade strategies. Treat as direct but era-limited game-market evidence, not as modern WNBA prop calibration.

### P0 — MLB doubles mechanism

**Observed production problem:** doubles props repeatedly reached WAIT while History Fit remained NR.

**R3 closure:** add an exact-market retrieval item using the already approved MLB Statcast expected-statistics source. xSLG assigns single/double/triple/HR probabilities to batted balls from comparable exit velocity/launch angle outcomes, with sprint speed included for some contact types. This closes a mechanism/retrieval gap only; direct sportsbook doubles-prop calibration remains unverified.

### P0 — MLB stolen-base opportunity mechanism

**Observed production problem:** stolen-base tails reached WAIT from book dispersion without enough independent structure.

**R3 closure:** add official Statcast catcher-throwing and baserunning models. Steal success depends on runner distance/speed plus pitcher/catcher and pitch context. A stolen-base prop additionally requires a chance to reach base and an attempt decision; historical SB rate alone is insufficient.

### P1 — MLB runs-scored opportunity / batting order

**Observed production problem:** runs-scored props were being treated too generically.

**R3 closure:** add batting-order mechanism evidence from Sokol (2003). Lineup sequence changes expected run production; for individual runs-scored props, batting slot and the surrounding on-base/power environment are opportunity inputs, not cosmetic lineup metadata. This is mechanism evidence, not direct sportsbook calibration.

### P1 — exact-market price-calibration gaps for doubles / stolen bases

**R3 action:** preserve an explicit GAP item. Tonight's search found useful MLB mechanism evidence but did not verify a robust direct closing-price calibration study for doubles or stolen-base props. The correct behavior is to retain a larger uncertainty margin rather than manufacture confidence.

## Deliberately not treated as closed

- WNBA closing player-prop calibration remains a direct research gap.
- NFL multi-book direct player-prop calibration remains a direct research gap.
- Boxing derivative markets remain a local-calibration gap.
- CFL broad pregame calibration remains thin.
- No universal numerical favorite/longshot haircut is promoted.
- No research item may become an independent BET vote, executable quote, fair-value rewrite, model-error rewrite, play-to rewrite, status rewrite or stake rewrite under R3.

## Promotion interpretation

R3 is a focused extension of the already-tested v1.8 candidate. It does not replace hard gates. Identity, freshness, exact-price verification, publication integrity and personnel-source enforcement remain code/contract concerns. R3 improves the knowledge layer so that History Fit can distinguish:

- genuine independent mechanism/support;
- market-structure caution;
- exact-market direct calibration;
- explicit local calibration gaps.

The intended production result is a cleaner evidence hierarchy, fewer forced analogies and less temptation to promote a sportsbook outlier into a serious candidate merely because the no-vig residual is positive.
