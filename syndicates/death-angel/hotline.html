The Evening at the Delphoria

The Louisville slip was still beside the phone when the dinner crowd started moving through the lobby. It had sat there since the afternoon because the only number on it was old. At six, a runner came through the front doors with the late sheet and the desk clerk lifted the stale slip before Jesse even reached the counter.

PHONE SLIP: LOUISVILLE FILE CLOSED // NOT CARRIED TO 18:15 // NO ACTION.

The new paper was thicker. One hundred seventy-four events behind it, enough names to make the Sports Desk look busy again. Jesse did not care how many there were. He cared whether any one of them deserved cash.

The first two were WNBA props. Nelson-Ododa showed +122 on one screen and −123 on another. Hamby showed +106 and −132. The gaps got the room's attention, the way a raised voice from the cocktail lounge always did. Jesse looked at the fair numbers and then at the disagreement underneath them. The first was short. The second barely cleared a midpoint before uncertainty swallowed the difference.

The Chargers came next at −105 when the fresh market made them +105 fair. The Sparks were +325 against +346. Two baseball dogs were short by sixteen and seventeen cents. Waverley finally reached +160 at Bet365—thirty cents better than DraftKings—and landed exactly on blended fair.

Jesse pushed the whole stack toward the closed side of the counter.

JESSE: “Seven slips. Seven no's. Put the phone away.”

Somebody behind the back-room door laughed at the timing. Jesse did not turn around. The elevator rattled upward, the lounge kept talking, and the late board ended with the same amount of money upstairs as it started with.
