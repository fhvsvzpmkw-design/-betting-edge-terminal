The Late Morning at the Delphoria

By half past nine the eight o'clock sheet had been pushed under the register, stained at one corner by a coffee cup and folded twice where the Giants number used to be. The cocktail lounge was not busy yet, but glassware was moving behind the bar and somebody in the back room had already started shuffling cards.

A runner came off the elevator with the new sheet and a separate phone slip. The Giants had finally reached +201. The clerk expected that one to stay open. Jesse looked at the fair number—+202—and slid the old sheet toward the wastebasket instead. A better price that still wasn't good enough did not interest him.

The second slip was stranger: an ATP doubles pair, Schnaitter/Wallner +256 at one window and only +175 at another. Jesse read the gap, then the word LEAN. He left that paper on the counter instead of taking it upstairs. The Toronto sheet came next with a different spread, +10.5, but the timestamps were already old.

Somebody from the lounge asked whether any of it meant action. Jesse didn't raise his voice.

JESSE: “One stays on the desk. One waits for a fresh number. The rest are finished. Don't call me with old paper.”
