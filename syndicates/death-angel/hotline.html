The Afternoon Went Quiet. Jesse Didn't.

Five afternoon passes disappeared into the clock. By dusk, the Sports Desk had a different board, the lounge had a different crowd, and Jesse Bains was still waiting for somebody to bring him a number worth keeping.

A

t 15:15 the Sports Desk still looked like a baseball room. The White Sox had improved to +158 and still weren't cheap enough. The Yankees had come down to −109 and still didn't clear. Miami had been squeezed from the long +200s down to +190. Milwaukee had been chased all the way to −172. The Sparks came back at −135. Five games, five passes, no ticket.

Toronto's old +10½ note stayed clipped beneath the morning sheet, but the exact market was gone. Jesse glanced at it once and left it there. Around the Delphoria, that was usually enough instruction.

Through the late afternoon the Yankees and Miami moved off the working board first, then the Sparks, then Milwaukee. The clerk crossed names out in pencil. Telephone slips piled up beside the ashtray. The cocktail lounge filled, emptied, and filled again. Downstairs, cards kept moving across green felt while upstairs the betting sheet got thinner.

Jesse was not handicapping any of it. He was doing what he always did when somebody put action in front of him: deciding whether he wanted his money attached to the story. Nothing from the afternoon survived that test.

Then the 18:08 feed came in. A runner brought four new names upstairs—Sultanes, Tacoma, Charros and Herediano—and put the fresh sheet beside the old one. Four more red PASS marks.

THE NAME BEHIND THE DESK

Regulars at the Delphoria know Jesse Bains is not the clerk and not the man making the line. He is the man people eventually ask for. The hotel, the lounge, the private rooms and the card game belong to the same old geography that made the name Death Angel travel farther than Jesse ever needed to explain it.

That history is why the Sports Desk works better when it looks ordinary. A clean hotel sheet on the counter. A phone behind the desk. A runner coming off the elevator. Somebody asking if Jesse has action. The danger is not printed in red ink; it is in how quickly the room understands the answer.

AROUND THE DELPHORIA

The lobby gets quieter after dinner. The cocktail lounge does not. A deck is cut downstairs. The elevator opens twice. Somebody leaves a folded racing form beside the Sports Desk and nobody claims it.

Jesse keeps the late sheet near the coffee, not because he likes it, but because the night is not over yet.

LAST WORD // JESSE BAINS
