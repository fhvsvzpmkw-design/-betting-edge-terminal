The Afternoon at the Delphoria

The morning slips were gone from under the glass by lunch. Seven files, seven refusals, no money sent anywhere. The desk clerk stacked them face down beside the telephone and went back to pretending the coffee was fresh.

By three the cocktail lounge had started making its own noise. A card game could be heard behind a door that never quite shut, the elevator coughed its way to another floor, and the phone behind the Sports Desk kept producing short messages from people who expected shorter answers.

The new sheet arrived with six names. Yankees −104 at the better shop, still short of −102 fair. Over 7.5 was −101 on one screen and −116 on the other, a useful difference that still didn't make the best price good. The three thinner files were worse. Jesse read the decisions, pushed each one to the finished side of the counter and kept moving.

The sixth slip stopped him. Louisville +130. One number. Bet365. Time beside it: 14:15. Jesse looked at the wall clock, then at the runner.

JESSE: “This number's old. Call when it's fresh.”

The slip stayed on top of the phone instead of going into the closed pile. Not action. Not interest. Just unfinished business until somebody brought back a current number.
