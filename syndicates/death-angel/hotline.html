The Morning at the Delphoria

The desk bell rang once at 9:24. The clerk looked at the man who had hit it, then at the sign that said SPORTS DESK in letters large enough to settle the question without conversation. Seven fresh slips came out of the envelope.

Five were stamped WAIT before they ever made it past the counter.

The first two had the kind of prices that got attention in the poker room. Sardarapat was +550 at one shop and +400 at the other. A draw in Iceland was +450 on one phone and +333 on the next. Somebody behind the wall stopped shuffling cards long enough to ask which number was real.

The clerk did not answer. He put both slips in the upstairs tray.

The worn elevator came down empty, opened, thought about it for a second, then went back up with a runner and the two papers. Jesse was at a table near the private room when they reached him.

RUNNER: “Big numbers.”
JESSE: “Different numbers.”

The runner waited.

JESSE: “Bring them back when the books agree.”

Two more slips came from baseball. Cody Freeman over half an RBI was +279 against +205. Osleivis Basabe over half a run was +190 against +137. Both needed official starting lineups before the sheet would let anybody move. Jesse read the word TBD twice and handed them back.
