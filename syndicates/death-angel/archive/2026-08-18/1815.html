The Afternoon Went Quiet. Jesse Didn't.

Five afternoon passes disappeared into the clock. By dusk, the Sports Desk had a different board, the lounge had a different crowd, and Jesse Bains was still waiting for somebody to bring him a number worth keeping.

t 15:15 the Sports Desk still looked like a baseball room. The White Sox were +158, the Yankees −109, Miami +190 and Milwaukee −172. The Sparks were −135. Five games, five passes, no ticket.

Then the clock started clearing the counter. Games began, old quotes stopped being usable, and the clerk crossed names off in pencil. Telephone slips collected beside the ashtray. The cocktail lounge changed crowds. Downstairs, the card game kept moving while the Sports Desk got thinner.

Jesse was not making a line. That was never his part of the operation. Somebody brought him the information; he decided whether he wanted his money attached to it. By late afternoon there was nothing left from the earlier sheet that he wanted.

At 18:08 the elevator opened and a runner brought the late feed upstairs: Sultanes, Tacoma, Charros and Herediano. Four new names. Four red PASS marks.
