The Evening at the Delphoria

The afternoon sheet had been dead for hours by the time the worn elevator started carrying dinner traffic upstairs. The counter clerk had filed the six 15:15 PASS slips in a drawer that stuck halfway out, poured the last of the coffee into a chipped cup, and stopped answering questions about whether Jesse was in the lounge or the back room. People who needed to know generally did.

Near six, the telephone rang twice and a runner came in behind it with the West Coast sheet folded under his coat. The cocktail lounge was louder now, cards clicking somewhere beyond the public room, smoke caught under the old ceiling fixtures. Jesse came down far enough to see the board without making a ceremony of it.

Golden State was the only familiar paper. Bet365 still showed +120; DraftKings had tightened to +114; the sheet's fair number was +126. Someone started to explain the home record. Jesse held up a hand before the sentence finished. Good team story, wrong enough price. The slip stayed on the counter only long enough to be marked PASS.

Vancouver and Portland came next. Both had better DraftKings numbers than Bet365—+120 instead of +108 on the Whitecaps −1.25, +125 instead of +115 on Portland −0.75. The clerk circled the shop differences in grease pencil. Jesse looked at the fair numbers, +125 and +132, and pushed both papers back. A better door to a bad room was still a bad room.

The Galaxy–San Jose under carried the kind of price Jesse dislikes on sight: Bet365 −125, DraftKings −135, fair −113. He did not ask for a second explanation. The last slip got more attention. Mexico was +320 at DraftKings and only +265 at Bet365, against a +314 fair estimate. Six cents on paper. The Betting Edge note underneath said the raw residual was only +1.4% before uncertainty, with no independent matchup or lineup signal verified.

Jesse folded that last slip once, tapped it against the counter, and put it beside the telephone instead of in his pocket. A man from the lounge asked if +320 meant they finally had action. The room went quiet enough to hear the elevator cables.

JESSE: “It means one book's longer. It doesn't mean I want it. Five papers. No action.”
