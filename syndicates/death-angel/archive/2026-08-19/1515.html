The Afternoon at the Delphoria

By three o'clock the hotel had changed shifts without ever looking cleaner. The coffee pot behind the counter had cooked down to something almost black, the cocktail lounge door kept opening and closing, and the elevator carried the same mixture of runners, card players and people who knew better than to ask who was upstairs.

The morning's best slip was still pinned beside the phone: Schnaitter/Wallner +256. It had been the only line Jesse had told the clerk not to throw away. A call came in just before the new sheet. The number was +234 now. Jesse looked at the old +250 threshold, tore the slip once down the middle and left both pieces beside the ashtray.

The next runner brought Toronto back, but not the Toronto sheet from morning. This one read +13.5 −112. The number was fresh. The fair line said −102. Jesse tapped the paper twice and pushed it back across the counter. Better spread, bad enough price.

The rest came in a stack. Giants +195, still short of +202 fair. Seattle +112, still short of +116. Athletics +168, barely a cent past +167 fair before any room for error. Golden State +120 while the made number sat at +129. Nothing survived the walk upstairs.

A man from the lounge asked whether six games meant six chances. The clerk stopped writing. Jesse did not look up.

JESSE: “Six papers. Same answer. Don't make me read them twice.”
