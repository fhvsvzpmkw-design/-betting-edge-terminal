The Evening at the Delphoria

By six o'clock the Delphoria had changed shifts without ever looking like it had one. The cocktail lounge was louder, the coffee at the counter was older, and the back-room poker game had been going long enough that nobody near the Sports Desk remembered who first sat down. The worn elevator kept stopping upstairs. The desk clerk kept writing telephone messages on carbon slips and sliding them into the same scarred tray.

The 15:15 betting sheet had left three WAITs on that tray. Two were already dead by the time Jesse came down: Inter Miami and Kansas City had crossed their start windows without becoming action. The third slip still had a name on it—Rhyne Howard, over 3.5 threes, +110 from the afternoon. The new sheet carried the exact same player, market and line. The price had changed to −105.

Jesse read the old number, then the new one. Fifteen cents gone. The sheet made the fresh fair +108 and put the current quote at negative value. Somebody at the counter started to explain that the player was still the same. Jesse looked up once.

JESSE: “I know who it is. Price is gone.”

The clerk moved the Howard slip to the closed pile.

Four new names were left on the late board. Phoenix wanted +280 when the sheet wanted +325. Dallas wanted favorite money in a preseason game that still had rotation questions. San Jose was +140 against a +154 fair number. Jesse pushed all three away without discussion.

The last paper was FC Dallas at Vancouver. Bet365 had +700. DraftKings had +550. The difference was wide enough that even the room behind the wall seemed quieter when the clerk read it aloud. The sheet showed +663 fair and a small nominal edge at the better shop, then stopped itself: 150 cents between the books, lineups unresolved, no bet. The instruction underneath wanted +800 or better after the books came back together and the starting lineups were real.

PHONE SLIP: FC DALLAS // +700 B365 / +550 DK // WAIT // NEED +800 + LINEUPS + FRESH CONVERGENCE // NO MONEY.

Jesse folded the slip once and left it beside the telephone. Not in his pocket. Not in the trash.

JESSE: “One stays by the phone. Everything else is finished.”

The lounge kept talking after that. The poker room kept moving money. The elevator groaned back toward the penthouse floor. At the Sports Desk, one slip remained open because the number needed proving, not because anybody had action.
