The Morning at the Delphoria

The late sheet from Wednesday was already filed when the first morning clerk came through the lobby. The coffee had been sitting too long, the phone slips were curled at the edges, and somebody had left a deck of cards on the back-room table as if the night shift planned to come back and finish the hand.

At six, five names went on the counter and none went upstairs. By eight, the same paper came back with cleaner numbers and one extra draw. San Francisco had finally found a fresh DraftKings quote at +180. Toronto had improved to +154. Both were closer. Jesse looked at the fair numbers beside them—+182 and +156—and pushed both slips back toward the clerk.

Atlanta's better DraftKings price had disappeared. St. Louis improved at Bet365 but lost the second shop. The Beşiktaş draw had been the only morning slip with a big-looking number at six; the +850 was gone now, leaving +750 against a +810 fair line. Jesse did not ask where it went.

The new paper was Lech–Thun. DraftKings showed +425, Bet365 +350, and the fair line sat at +424. Somebody at the desk pointed at the one-cent difference like it meant something. Jesse stared at him until he stopped.

JESSE: “Better paper. Same answer. Six of 'em. No action.”
