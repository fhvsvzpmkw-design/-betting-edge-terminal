The Evening at the Delphoria

The afternoon sheet had gone quiet before the lobby did. By the time the dinner crowd pushed through the front doors, all seven 15:15 files were locked or underway and there was nothing left at the Sports Desk that Jesse could still buy. The counter clerk stacked the dead slips under the telephone and turned the top page face down.

Behind the lobby wall, the Delphoria kept its usual second life. Glasses knocked together in the cocktail lounge. A chair scraped in the back room where money and cards had been moving long before anybody bothered printing a sports sheet. The elevator groaned between floors. Every few minutes somebody crossed the lobby without looking toward the counter, which was how people looked at the counter when they knew exactly who was upstairs.

Jesse came down late. No speech, no entourage at the desk, just the Death Angel taking the new paper from the clerk and reading it standing up. Five of the six slips died fast. Arizona cost more than fair. San Diego was close and still too expensive. Seattle wanted favorite money in a near-even market. The Dodgers charged a premium for being the Dodgers. The Vancouver WNBA game had a local story and a number below the price the sheet required. Jesse pushed each one to the closed side.

The last slip stayed in his hand. Central Coast: +650 at DraftKings, +400 at Bet365. Two hundred fifty cents between the books. The paper made +546 fair and showed a big raw residual at the better shop, then put the word WAIT across the top because the market did not agree with itself and the roster still needed confirmation.

PHONE SLIP: CENTRAL COAST +650 // WAIT // CHECK MARKET + ROSTER // +650 OR BETTER // NO MONEY.

Jesse looked at the two prices again. He did not ask why they were different. He did not pretend he had a better model than the sheet. He put the slip beside the phone instead of in the trash.

JESSE: “One stays open. Nobody touches it.”

The clerk nodded. The lounge kept talking. Somewhere behind the door the poker game moved on to another hand. At the Sports Desk, five slips were closed and one sat under the telephone waiting for something more useful than a story.
