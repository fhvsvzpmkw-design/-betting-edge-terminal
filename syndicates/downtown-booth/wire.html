BILL “THE NUMBER” WESTON

Weston doesn't work the sportsbook floor. He works upstairs, usually before the windows get busy, with yesterday's sheets stacked beside the phone and a legal pad full of numbers nobody at the book has seen yet.

The story around town is that he learned early that the loudest guy in the room usually knew the least. So Weston became the opposite: no tout board, no victory laps, no need to have action every night. His value to the syndicate is making a number before the market gets a chance to influence him, then documenting exactly where the disagreement begins and where it disappears.

His sheets are intentionally plain. A fax can travel. A fax can be folded into a jacket. A fax doesn't need to explain itself to anybody who wasn't supposed to receive it.

Don't tell me who you like. Tell me the number where you like them.
WESTON METHOD // MORNING NOTE

THE PRICE HAS TO BE WRONG — NOT JUST DIFFERENT

A line shop can find the best available number and still find no wager. The useful question is not whether one book is cheaper than another. It is whether the best executable transaction clears fair value by enough to survive model error, incomplete information and ordinary market noise.

This morning four candidates made the private sheet. All four failed that test. That is information. It is not inactivity.

The hardest number to write on a sheet is zero. Write it anyway.
