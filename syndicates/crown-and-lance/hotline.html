The Doors Open

Today's star: Alex Daventry. The Crown & Lance will not admit an audience for hours, but at nine in the morning it already has a tournament problem. The red gallery's pennants have been hung over the blue gallery, the arena gate stops six inches above the sand, and the stage manager has announced that the missing winch crank was “definitely here yesterday.”

Alex spent enough of his youth as Gwydion learning chores, clocks and the consequences of moving too early to trust “definitely here yesterday” as an inventory system. He asks for the prop list instead.

Chicken & First Pour

The kitchen is the one department having a good morning. The chicken count matches the reservation sheet, the potatoes are where potatoes ought to be, and the first bread trays are cooling beside the service line.

The wine captain is less fortunate. Someone has stacked the blue-gallery goblets behind the red-gallery service station, apparently on the theory that this will “balance out” the pennant error.

“Don't balance it. Put each thing where it belongs.”

Alex walks the arena route with the prop list. The gate crank is not on the wall, not beside the winch and not in the repair bin. A squire remembers carrying “a brass thing” into costume storage because it looked decorative.

Alex does not ask the squire to define decorative. Some questions make a morning longer.

The crank is hanging from a peg beside three ceremonial belts and a wooden key nobody has used since the summer show rewrite. Alex takes the crank and leaves the wooden key exactly where it is. Old adventure habits encourage keeping strange objects. Running a live show teaches him that sometimes the correct inventory decision is to stop collecting them.

Between Bouts

The repaired arena gate needs ten minutes before the next test. Alex takes the folded VigScope sheet through the service door and sits on an upturned crate behind the royal box.

The host voice disappears.

Three WAITs. Four PASSes. No BET.

Roma–Fiorentina first-half over 3.5 gets the second look because Bet365 is showing +1800. Then he sees DraftKings at +1340 and the instruction to wait for fresh book convergence plus lineup and tactical confirmation.

“Big number. Not a finished number.”

Zandalasini over 2.5 rebounds is +132 at DraftKings and −116 at Bet365, with participation, role and book agreement still unresolved. Conine over 0.5 runs remains +240 at Bet365 but still needs the starting lineup and matchup review.

Ruiz is easier. The earlier +190 WAIT price is gone; +159 is now PASS. Alex crosses it off. The two soccer totals and Neom moneyline are already PASS.

He folds the sheet again.

“Three holds. Nothing to bet.”

The gate test is ready. The gambler goes back in his coat pocket with the paper; the host returns to the floor.

Back to the Floor

The gate comes down cleanly on the next test. The red pennants return to the red gallery. The blue goblets return to the blue service station. The stage manager circles three items on the prop list and, for once, does not use the phrase “probably backstage.”

At eleven, the first knights arrive for fight rehearsal. One discovers that his helmet plume has been packed with the kitchen linens. Alex looks at the helmet, looks at the linens and decides this is at least a problem with an obvious route.

By lunch the chicken is roasting. Wine bottles are lined beneath the service counters but remain corked. The audience will get noise, banners, horses, dinner and enough theatrical certainty to believe the entire performance was planned that way.

The sports sheet stays folded behind the royal box.

There is nothing on it that deserves to interrupt the show yet.
