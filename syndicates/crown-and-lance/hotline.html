The Doors Open

Tonight's star: Alex Daventry. Before the casino lights and the green coat, he spent his youth answering to Gwydion in Llewdor, escaped a wizard with unreasonable household expectations, survived a pirate trip, discovered he was actually Prince Alexander of Daventry, and somehow turned all of that into a career in live entertainment.

These days Alex hosts the Crown & Lance, where his duties include keeping knights on cue, chicken on the tables, wine in the goblets and tourists reasonably convinced the dragon is supposed to smoke. He considers this a surprisingly logical second career.

TONIGHT AT THE CROWN & LANCE · CHICKEN ON THE TABLE · WINE IN THE GOBLETS · KNIGHTS IN THE LISTS · ALEX ON THE FLOOR

Chicken & First Pour

The first chicken plates hit the tables before the opening trumpet has finished echoing. Servers move down the rows with bread, potatoes and heavy goblets while the house wine follows behind them. The red-banner section immediately begins chanting for a knight who has not entered yet.

Alex has seen this problem before in different clothes: everybody wants the interesting part before the conditions are ready.

A server stops him near the west gallery and hands him a small brass key found underneath the wine cart. Nobody knows what it opens. Alex turns it over once and slips it into his belt pouch.

“If you don't know what the key opens, that is not the same thing as the key being useless.”

The server stares at him. Alex takes a sip of wine, checks the show clock and sends the second chicken cart toward the north tables.

Between Bouts

The first course is mostly cleared when Alex finally gets four quiet minutes. He slips through the service door behind the red-banner tables, drops the show smile and pulls the folded VigScope sheet from inside his coat.

This is the part the crowd never sees. No trumpets. No prince. No dragon. Just a sports gambler in Vegas checking whether any of the numbers have become real.

He starts with the two soccer prices because +550 and +450 will make anybody look twice. Then he sees the other shops: +400 and +333. Alex does not need another speech. The sheet still says WAIT.

Freeman and Basabe are still waiting on lineups and better numbers. Flau’jae Johnson is still short of even money. Austin and the Cubs have already been crossed into PASS. Five holds. Two passes. Nothing to bet.

Alex folds the sheet once. “All right. Back to the horses.”

His break lasts about forty seconds longer.

The stage manager appears in the doorway. The wooden dragon will not roll, the smoke cue is early and the release pin for the arena gate is missing. Alex puts the VigScope sheet back inside his coat. The gambler disappears; the host is back on duty.

The brass key from under the wine cart opens a battered cabinet beneath the west stair. Inside are two replacement pins, a coil of red cord and something labeled DRAGON — DO NOT LOSE.

Alex hands over the pin.

“This is why we keep things.”

By the next charge, the dragon moves, the smoke is approximately where it belongs and the red-banner section is pounding goblets on the table again.

The Second Pour

The wine comes around again while the chicken plates are being cleared. Alex walks the edge of the arena, greeting tables, checking the next entrance and making sure the west stair is actually locked this time.

He has spent enough of his life around castles to know that the impressive part is rarely the important part. Banners are impressive. Trumpets are impressive. A door that closes when it is supposed to is important.

Somebody from the kitchen asks whether the final chicken tray can go out before the next charge. Alex looks at the clock.

“Now.”

The timing works. The tray clears the aisle. The horses come through. Nobody gets flattened by dinner.

For Alex, this qualifies as an excellent performance.
