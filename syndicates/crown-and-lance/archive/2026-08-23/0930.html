The Doors Open

The first trumpet went off before the kitchens were ready, which is how the Crown & Lance begins most respectable mornings. Two pages ran toward the lists carrying seven names, one steward ran the other direction carrying a tray of bread, and Alex Daventry stood in the middle of it all with the expression of a man who had once learned that moving too early could get him turned into something unpleasant.

“Five at the gate,” he told the herald. “None through it.” The galleries booed. The horses did not care. Somewhere behind the curtain, a cook dropped a pot.

Alex's Table

A bowl of porridge appeared beside Alex's program. He stared at it longer than the situation deserved.

“Take that back to the kitchen.”

The young steward asked why.

Alex looked toward the royal box. “Experience.”

Years in Manannan's house had left him with an unusual respect for clocks, naps, locked doors and meals whose ingredients he had not personally witnessed. It also left him with very little sympathy for a cheering crowd that wanted every interesting number declared a contest immediately. A door being open, he had learned, did not mean it was time to walk through it.

Between Bouts

With nobody actually permitted into the lists, the production department attempted to create excitement on its own. Two squires dragged a wooden dragon onto the arena floor. One wheel fell off before it reached center stage.

Alex watched it lean sideways. “Rosella is going to say something about this.”

From the gallery came a crash, followed by the unmistakable sound of somebody insisting the crash was part of the show. A tabby cat emerged from behind the kitchen curtain with a length of red ribbon in its mouth.

Alex put down his program.

“Absolutely not.”

The cat ignored him, which at least felt familiar.

By the time the next course arrived, the pirate-themed serving crew had somehow lost a purse, the west gallery had adopted Sardarapat as its champion despite explicit instructions not to, and the master of horses wanted to know whether the wooden dragon counted as livestock. Alex gave him the same answer he had given the herald: not yet.

The Royal Box

King Graham arrived late enough to miss the broken dragon and early enough to be told about it by three different people. Queen Valanice took one look at the arena and quietly moved the royal table farther from the aisle.

Rosella entered last, saw the wheel leaning against the curtain, and looked directly at her brother.

“Your dragon seems tired.”

“It's waiting on confirmation.”

She laughed hard enough that the herald lost his place.
