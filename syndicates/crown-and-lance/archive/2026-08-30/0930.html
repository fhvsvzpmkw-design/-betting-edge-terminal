The Doors Open

Alex Daventry hosts The Crown & Lance with the calm of somebody who learned very young that clocks, keys and apparently useless objects tend to matter later. His stage role carries the Gwydion / Prince Alexander history into a modern Vegas dinner show, but the job this morning is simpler: get a Sunday matinee through the first entrance without losing a horse, a tray or the audience.

Five minutes before the opening trumpet, the brass gate key is missing. The red gallery has blue-section goblets, one chicken rack is parked behind the wrong curtain, and a steward keeps insisting everything is “almost ready.” Alex has heard that phrase before.

“Almost ready is how you end up locked on the wrong side of a door. Hold the gate.”

Chicken & First Pour

The kitchen sends the chicken rack to the correct service aisle while Alex walks the route backward: gate, prop table, banner stand, steward station. Years ago, under Manannan, timing a room mattered more than looking heroic. The instinct still works in a casino show.

The missing key turns up clipped beneath the blue pennant where a hurried page had hung it “for safekeeping.” Alex says nothing for three seconds, which is enough. The goblets cross back to the proper gallery. The first pour catches up. The trumpet gets a new cue.

By the time the knights enter the floor, the audience sees exactly what it paid to see. Nobody in the gallery needs to know that the entire kingdom was delayed by a key sitting six feet from the lock.

Final Fanfare

The second trumpet sounds before Alex reaches the curtain. A knight is waiting for the gate cue, a server has discovered two extra goblets, and the page who hid the key has decided not to explain his reasoning.

Alex takes the key, checks the clock and opens the gate on time. The galleries cheer because that is what galleries do when the machinery stays invisible.

He has no sports ticket in his pocket. He has one price to remember and a live show that is already moving again.
