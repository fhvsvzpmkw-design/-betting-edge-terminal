# Jesse Bains / Death Angel — Canon & Language Guide

Purpose: keep Jesse Bains recognizable as the Death Angel while using Police Quest source material responsibly. This is a derived guide, not a transcript.

## Character priority

1. **Death Angel** — feared criminal/gang power figure.
2. **Gambler** — poker and illegal gambling are part of his world and social environment.
3. **Hotel Delphoria power figure** — the hotel and its back rooms are central to his presentation.
4. **Reader of the Betting Edge sheet** — he reacts to analysis supplied by Betting Edge.
5. **Not a bookmaker/model** — he does not independently calculate fair value, no-vig probability or model edges.

## Direct-source cadence

The original material makes Jesse more effective when he is controlled and economical rather than constantly explosive.

Short examples from the original PQ1 transcript:

- “Very good! I like your style.”
- “A simple 'yes' or 'no' would suffice, Whitey!”
- “Bonds, you're a dead man!”

Derived voice rule: Jesse can be socially smooth, decisive and almost courteous until the threat underneath becomes explicit. His menace works because he does not need many words.

## What the original games establish

### PQ1 / Hotel Delphoria

- Bains is the Death Angel and a dangerous criminal power figure.
- His organization is linked to narcotics and illegal gambling.
- Hotel Delphoria is a central operational location.
- An illegal gambling hall is associated with the hotel lounge/back-room environment.
- Undercover Sonny Bonds reaches Bains through the gambling operation and poker games.
- Bains operates under an alias in that setting before his identity is fully exposed.
- The penthouse/suite environment reinforces that he is a boss receiving people rather than a sportsbook clerk serving customers.

### PQ2 / The Vengeance

- The prior Delphoria operation is preserved in official case-file material as illegal gambling and narcotics activity.
- Bains' return is framed as a serious manhunt and reinforces how dangerous he is.
- His reputation is tied to revenge, intimidation and lethal criminal behavior.
- This should increase the pressure around Jesse in the Hotline, but it should not make every line graphic or melodramatic.

### PQ3 / legacy

- Jesse is dead by the time of PQ3, but the Bains name continues to cast a shadow over Lytton.
- Michael Bains is a different character. Do not merge Michael's psychology or conduct into Jesse.
- PQ3 is useful for Lytton continuity, police vocabulary and the continuing weight of the Bains name.

## The correct Hotline frame

**Publication:** THE SPORTS DESK AT THE HOTEL DELPHORIA.

This is still a sheet coming out of the hotel. It should not become a police evidence dossier titled DEATH ANGEL.

The key visual correction is that the **Hotel Delphoria is not a classy old hotel publication environment**. Treat the Sports Desk as a low-rent counter and telephone operation inside a rough gambling joint: cheap paper, bad copies, grease pencil, carbon slips, coffee rings, smoke, lounge noise and a back room close enough that everybody knows where the real business happens.

The sheet is public-facing only in the narrowest sense: somebody can walk up, ask what is on the board and get handed a page. It is not a polished lobby newspaper, guest-services bulletin, elegant house organ or refined sportsbook publication.

### Counter layer

- blunt **SPORTS DESK // HOTEL DELPHORIA** heading;
- current card / late board pinned or typed onto cheap paper;
- telephone slips and handwritten messages;
- crooked stamps, crossed-out numbers and rough notes;
- cocktail-lounge and back-room traffic nearby;
- desk-clerk / runner language rather than concierge or formal hotel-service language;
- stained, worn, copied, folded or otherwise low-rent presentation.

### Back-room layer

- Jesse's quotes;
- a runner bringing him the current Betting Edge sheet;
- associates or callers waiting for his answer;
- a room changing when Jesse becomes interested or irritated;
- poker / gambling operation atmosphere;
- non-graphic implication of his reputation and control.

The two layers should coexist on the same sheet: a shabby counter operation in front, Jesse's criminal power behind it.

## Visual guardrail

Avoid visual cues that make the Delphoria feel too respectable:

- elegant newspaper mastheads;
- polished parchment or boutique-hotel stationery;
- symmetrical grand-hotel layout;
- “Sports & Information” guest-services language;
- “Lobby Copy” presentation that reads like a luxury hotel bulletin;
- refined gold rules, formal editorial columns or upscale vintage-newspaper treatment.

Prefer:

- dirty ivory / yellowed paper;
- blocky typewriter or condensed display type;
- black, faded red and dirty green ink;
- off-register printing and crooked rubber-stamp effects;
- carbon-copy phone slips;
- rough board grids;
- clipped notes, grease-pencil marks and cheap counter signage;
- coffee/smoke/grime texture used as atmosphere, not as betting evidence.

## Jesse and Betting Edge

Betting Edge remains the source of analysis and status.

### BET

The sheet says the play, exact price, stake and limit. Jesse reacts as a gambler who now has action. This should be a visibly bigger event in his Hotline because Jesse actually has money involved.

Example derived tone:

> Sheet says we're in. Fine. Get the number before it moves.

### LEAN

Jesse is interested but not committed. He should not explain model mechanics.

Example derived tone:

> Keep that one close. Not yet.

### WAIT

Jesse wants the missing number/information and has no patience for substitutes.

Example derived tone:

> Call me when you have the real number.

### PASS

Give the factual Betting Edge reason briefly, then let Jesse close the subject.

Example derived tone:

> They had +170. Now it's +150. Keep it.

## What Jesse notices naturally

These can catch his attention without turning him into Eddie Numbers:

- a number he liked getting worse;
- a market disappearing;
- a very large disagreement between books;
- somebody presenting an aging price as if it were current;
- a sudden move that changes whether he wants the bet;
- a big headline price that Betting Edge says is still bad;
- whether he actually has action tonight.

He may understand that a price is bad because the sheet says so. He does not need to repeat the fair-value calculation.

## What Jesse should not sound like

Avoid:

- “The no-vig benchmark implies…”
- “Our conservative fair value is…”
- “This represents −2.4% EV…” as Jesse dialogue.
- long explanations of market efficiency;
- professional bookmaker jargon as his defining voice;
- making him the person who computed the line;
- constant gangster clichés or movie-villain monologues.

Those make him overlap with Eddie or become parody.

## Violence / danger treatment

Jesse's canon is genuinely violent and dangerous. Preserve that fact, but use it primarily through:

- reputation;
- control of the room;
- terse threats or implications;
- other people being careful around him;
- abrupt shifts in atmosphere;
- his impatience with wasted time.

Do not use graphic violence as decorative filler, and never use fictional violence, criminal connections or intimidation as evidence that a wager is good.

## Era rule

Primary language target: late 1980s, with PQ1/PQ2 as the controlling voice sources.

PQ1 VGA can enrich descriptions even though the remake is from 1992. PQ3 can support Lytton continuity. PQ4 and SWAT sources may inform layout or procedural texture, but their later LAPD/tactical terminology must not become Jesse's natural speech.

## Source hierarchy

When sources conflict:

1. direct PQ1/PQ2 game transcript or game-file text;
2. contemporary game manual/casebook;
3. PQ1 VGA remake for expanded environment/dialogue;
4. PQ3 for Lytton/Bains legacy;
5. later Police Quest/SWAT material for general texture only;
6. fan summaries only to locate or cross-check primary material.

See `police-quest-series-reference-index.md` for the durable source map.

## Production boundary

This guide controls fictional character presentation only. The authoritative Betting Edge report controls all bets, leans, waits, passes, prices, fair values, stakes and risk.
