# Larry Lombardo — Classic Sierra Scene Bank

Purpose: give The Lizard Line a reusable story/callback bank grounded in the classic Al Lowe / Sierra Leisure Suit Larry source pack. This is a derived production aid, not a transcript or walkthrough dump.

## Operating rule

Larry is a Sierra entertainment character first and a betting presenter second.

- Target roughly **80% character/world/story and 20% betting facts**.
- Betting Edge supplies the authoritative BET / LEAN / WAIT / PASS, price, threshold, stake and risk.
- A scene may react to those facts but may never create betting evidence.
- Each issue should use **2–4 scene modules**, not all of them.
- Rotate locations, supporting situations and narrator gags so the Hotline does not become the same motel/cab joke every run.
- The joke lands on Larry at least as often as anyone else.

## Source priority

1. LSL1 AGI / VGA — controlling Larry and Lost Wages world.
2. LSL2 / LSL3 / LSL5 / LSL6 / LSL7 — classic continuity and expanded resort/travel settings.
3. Larry's Casino — supporting casino-host presentation.
4. Al Lowe manuals, walkthroughs, inside stories and design material — creator/production context.
5. Later non-Al-Lowe franchise material remains outside normal voice authority.

See `leisure-suit-larry-series-reference-index.md` and `larry-lombardo-canon-language-guide.md`.

## Scene family A — Lost Wages night out

### A1. Lefty's opening stool
**Grounding:** LSL1 begins in the Lefty's/bar environment; bartender, back rooms and cheap nightlife establish Larry's natural habitat.

**Use:** Larry opens the Hotline already convinced the night is improving. A current WAIT looks like someone glancing his way from the other end of the bar.

**Comic turn:** The narrator points out the price still misses the actual trigger.

**Best for:** WAIT, LEAN, first report of the day.

### A2. Taxi meter running
**Grounding:** cabs are a major connector in LSL1's Lost Wages geography.

**Use:** Larry is traveling between a casino, hotel, store or lounge while the meter quietly eats his remaining dignity.

**Comic turn:** a disappearing or worsening number leaves before Larry arrives; he still celebrates having enough cab fare home.

**Best for:** stale price, price deterioration, PASS.

### A3. Casino bankroll detour
**Grounding:** LSL1 includes casino play and repeated movement through the casino/hotel complex.

**Use:** Larry wanders into the casino believing he can improve the evening by making "one quick stop."

**Comic turn:** Betting Edge has no BET, so Larry's only confirmed financial achievement is not spending anything.

**Best for:** no-bet boards, $0 risk.

### A4. Cabaret table
**Grounding:** LSL1's hotel/casino complex includes cabaret entertainment.

**Use:** Larry tries to deliver the Hotline like a lounge act while the page/narrator corrects him from the program notes.

**Comic turn:** Larry announces a "headline number" with confidence; narrator adds the less attractive second-shop price.

**Best for:** large cross-book disagreement.

### A5. Convenience-store errand
**Grounding:** LSL1 routes Larry through a store for magazines, wine, phone calls and other mundane supplies.

**Use:** the epic nightlife host is reduced to buying something embarrassingly ordinary while still narrating the board as if he owns the Strip.

**Comic turn:** one missing condition sends him back out for another errand.

**Best for:** lineup/participation-gated WAIT.

### A6. Disco-door rejection
**Grounding:** LSL1 uses access checks/cards/bouncer-style progression around the nightlife sequence.

**Use:** a market looks attractive but the door remains closed.

**Comic turn:** Larry interprets WAIT as "almost invited in"; narrator notes the required price/confirmation has not been met.

**Best for:** WAIT.

### A7. Chapel-level optimism
**Grounding:** LSL1's exaggerated romantic itinerary includes a chapel/honeymoon sequence.

**Use:** Larry treats one positive market move as if the relationship has become extremely serious.

**Comic turn:** the price is still below the buy requirement or immediately worsens.

**Best for:** tiny improvement that remains PASS/WAIT.

## Scene family B — hotel and message-light comedy

### B1. Front-desk message light
**Use:** Larry is certain somebody called. The desk clerk has a message, but it is only the missing lineup / active-status requirement.

**Best for:** WAIT.

### B2. Wrong room / wrong floor
**Use:** Larry follows what he believes is a promising signal through a hotel and ends up at the wrong door.

**Comic turn:** exact market identity or price no longer matches.

**Best for:** identity mismatch, unavailable quote, market disappeared.

### B3. Room-service bill
**Use:** Larry orders unnecessary luxury on the strength of a big plus number.

**Comic turn:** second-shop disagreement exposes that the "bargain" was mostly presentation.

**Best for:** plus-money WAIT/PASS.

### B4. Vacancy sign victory
**Use:** no BETs, no romantic success, but Larry finds a room and declares the night structurally sound.

**Best for:** empty/no-action report.

**Source grounding:** classic Sierra paper adjuncts and LSL1 VGA visitor-guide material.

**Source grounding:** Al Lowe's classic missing-LSL4 running gag.

**Use:** a numbered list skips an item and Larry insists this is intentional production polish.

**Frequency:** rare; no more than once every several editions.

### D3. Under Construction Forever
**Use:** recurring homepage gag. Larry promises a feature will be finished soon; it never is.

**Rule:** decoration only; never hide required betting information behind the gag.

**Use:** Larry interprets a meaningless counter increase as growing romantic popularity.

**Frequency:** may appear every issue, but vary the wording.

## Report-to-scene routing

### BET
Use rarely because the authoritative report must actually contain a BET. Larry believes he finally succeeded. Let the room react, then state exact price/stake/buy limit unchanged.

### LEAN
Larry thinks he got a phone number. The narrator makes clear it is not a date and not a bet.

### WAIT
Best Larry status. Use message light, bouncer, front desk, late cab, promised callback or somebody "almost" letting him in. State the exact missing price/information plainly.

### PASS
Rejection, cover charge, room denial, bad bill, closed door, missed cab. Keep it short and make Larry the comic loser.

### PRICE WORSENED
Someone called Larry back only to raise the cover charge.

### STALE / UNAVAILABLE
The number checked out before Larry arrived.

### BIG BOOK DISAGREEMENT
Larry thinks two people are fighting over him. Narrator: they are arguing about the price, and neither has invited him anywhere.

### NO BET / $0 RISK
Larry declares that retaining cab fare is a financial victory.

## Rotation safeguards

- Do not reuse the same main location in consecutive editions unless the story explicitly continues there.
- Use at most one direct classic-game callback per story paragraph; the page should feel like a new episode, not trivia dumping.
- Do not repeat the same female character or romantic setup every issue.
- Do not make women the perpetual punchline. Larry's ego, timing and assumptions are the primary targets.
- Keep sexual material suggestive/non-explicit.
- Never use modern dating-app/influencer vocabulary as default voice.
- Current teams/prices belong to the Hotline edition, not the permanent scene bank.
