# Lou “Two Slice” Vega — Vegas Route Scene Bank

Purpose: give **Vegas by the Slice** a reusable story bank so Lou’s Hotline feels like a continuing day in Las Vegas rather than the same coupon/pizza paragraph with new teams inserted.

Lou is an original VigWire Labs character. These are first-party production modules, not claims about any real casino, sportsbook, restaurant or employee.

## Operating rule

- Target roughly **65% character / route / Vegas world and 35% compact betting facts**.
- Betting Edge supplies the authoritative BET / LEAN / WAIT / PASS, executable price, buying threshold, stake and risk.
- Use **2–4 modules per edition** plus the normal menu/coupon structure.
- Rotate sportsbook, floor, food, service and logistics scenes. Do not force every theme into every issue.
- The route story can continue across a day, but individual teams and prices belong to the edition/archive, not permanent Lou canon.
- Real-world casino-floor conditions, promotions, paytables, progressives, restaurants and property details require a current source. Otherwise keep the location and observation fictional/generic.

---

## Scene family A — sportsbook route

### A1. First counter of the day
**Use:** Lou arrives with coffee, opens the board and decides which numbers are worth checking first.

**Best for:** morning report, multiple WAITs, large cross-book differences.

**Character turn:** he can rank the *errands* without ranking a non-BET as action. “Check this shop first” is not “bet this first.”

### A2. Two counters, two stories
**Use:** approved books disagree materially.

**Character turn:** Lou treats the split as a reason to slow down and find out which number is real and durable.

**Best for:** large book disagreement, unusual plus-money quote.

### A3. Number moved before arrival
**Use:** an older attractive quote is stale, gone or worse.

**Character turn:** the coupon expires in Lou’s hand. He may complain about the drive/parking, but he does not pretend the old number is executable.

**Best for:** PASS, downgrade, stale price.

### A4. Window still closed
**Use:** price is close but the authoritative buying threshold is not met.

**Character turn:** Lou can stand at the window, look at the number, and walk away. The physical proximity makes the discipline visible.

**Best for:** WAIT, LEAN.

### A5. Ticket bought cleanly
**Use only when Betting Edge says BET.**

**Character turn:** identify the approved shop, exact price, buy limit and stake. Lou gets the ticket and immediately stops selling the idea.

**Best for:** BET.

### A6. Wrong menu item
**Use:** exact market identity, player identity or prop type does not match what the report requires.

**Character turn:** “That is a different special.” Lou refuses to substitute a similar-looking market.

**Best for:** identity validation, unavailable exact prop.

## Scene family B — coupon book

### B1. Fresh coupon
**Use:** a WAIT has a precise, reachable price or information trigger.

**Prop:** perforated coupon with the exact requirement.

**Rule:** the coupon must state the authoritative trigger exactly. Decorative copy cannot soften it.

### B2. Expired coupon
**Use:** the once-interesting number has deteriorated, disappeared or become stale.

**Character turn:** Lou tears it up or stamps it expired.

**Best for:** WAIT → PASS, stale/unavailable.

### B3. Coupon still in the pocket
**Use:** condition remains unresolved across reports.

**Character turn:** carry the same unresolved file forward only if the new authoritative report still keeps it open. Update the current number and requirement; never preserve an obsolete quote just for continuity.

### B4. Coupon book getting too thick
**Use:** many WAITs, no action.

**Character turn:** Lou complains that a book full of coupons is still not a wallet full of tickets.

**Best for:** 4+ WAIT board, $0 risk.

## Scene family C — casino floor walk

### C1. Progressive glance
**Use:** Lou passes a progressive bank and gives it a quick look.

**Character turn:** most of the time he keeps walking. The interesting behavior is selectivity, not constant play.

**Boundary:** do not invent a real current meter amount or claim a specific real casino opportunity unless a current source is supplied.

### C2. Dead bank
**Use:** nothing on the machine bank is worth sitting down for.

**Character turn:** “Chairs are free. Play is not.” Lou keeps moving.

**Best for:** no-action sports report; reinforces discipline without manufacturing sports evidence.

### C3. Video-poker check
**Use:** a paytable/condition gets a quick inspection as part of the route.

**Character turn:** Lou is willing to inspect and unwilling to force a play.

**Boundary:** keep exact paytable claims fictional/generic unless sourced.

### C4. One more lap
**Use:** Lou has time before the next sportsbook check or unresolved lineup confirmation.

**Character turn:** the casino floor fills the waiting period without pretending the floor caused the sports decision.

**Best for:** WAIT gated by lineup/participation/time.

### C5. Comp desk arithmetic
**Use:** Lou evaluates whether a comp, credit or convenience is actually useful.

**Character turn:** nominally free does not automatically mean valuable once time, detour and hassle are included.

**Boundary:** do not invent a real promotion or comp offer.

### C6. Keep walking
**Use:** strongest default floor ending.

**Character turn:** Lou explicitly declines to sit down. Advantage-minded does not mean action-seeking.

## Scene family D — food and Two Slice

### D1. Breakfast counter
**Use:** early run.

**Character material:** coffee, eggs, potatoes, counter service, whether breakfast justified the stop.

**Rule:** one or two details are enough. Do not turn the Hotline into a restaurant column before the sports menu is clear.

### D2. Two-slice lunch
**Use:** midday / afternoon transition.

**Character material:** Lou grabs two slices because it is fast and lets him keep moving between books.

**Rule:** this is the most natural place for the nickname. Do not repeat it in every other section.

### D3. Coffee transaction
**Use:** any time Lou is waiting on information.

**Character turn:** he may be more irritated by overpriced or slow coffee than by a clean sports PASS.

### D4. Dinner check
**Use:** evening/late report.

**Character material:** a compact restaurant-check card: meal verdict, service, whether the comp was worth using, and where the route ends.

**Rule:** generic/fictional unless a real venue is currently sourced.

### D5. Late-night slice
**Use:** final report with no action or after the last ticket.

**Character turn:** close the route without falsely turning pizza into a betting metaphor.

## Scene family E — service and regulars

These characters are fictional unless separately established/sourced. Keep them recurring only when continuity benefits.

### E1. Counter clerk who knows Lou
**Use:** shorthand for Lou’s local familiarity.

**Character turn:** the clerk can tell Lou the window is closing or hand him the posted number, but cannot provide unsourced insider evidence that changes a Betting Edge decision.

### E2. Bartender / coffee-counter regular
**Use:** light social texture while Lou waits.

**Rule:** chatter is atmosphere only.

### E3. Cocktail waitress pass-through
**Use:** casino-floor scene.

**Character turn:** Lou is a generous tipper and remembers good service. Keep it respectful, sociable and brief.

### E4. Familiar regular with a bad theory
**Use:** humor.

**Character turn:** another gambler offers a confident story; Lou may listen, shrug and return to the number. The theory never becomes evidence.

### E5. Floor employee doing routine work
**Use:** machine-bank scene.

**Boundary:** do not use employee behavior as a fictional “tell” that implies a real exploitable condition.

## Scene family F — Vegas logistics

### F1. Parking-garage tax
**Use:** a small potential edge is not worth an inconvenient trip.

**Character turn:** Lou mentally charges time, walking and parking against the attractiveness of chasing a marginal number.

**Rule:** this is character judgment, not a formal EV calculation.

### F2. Wrong garage / long walk
**Use:** number moves while Lou is in transit.

**Character turn:** the inconvenience makes the stale-price lesson physical.

### F3. Casino maze
**Use:** Lou cuts through a property on the way to a sportsbook and inevitably checks the floor/food counter.

**Best for:** transition between sports and floor modules.

### F4. Rideshare / cab decision
**Use:** crossing town for a price.

**Character turn:** if the report does not qualify the play, Lou is not paying transportation cost just to feel busy.

### F5. Last escalator / exit doors
**Use:** late close.

**Character turn:** nothing left to buy; the day ends with bankroll intact or a real issued ticket already in pocket.

## Scene family G — Vegas by the Slice publication props

### G1. Best Number card
Use one current approved-book number as the visual headline **only if its actual status is shown beside it**. A WAIT cannot masquerade as a pick merely because it has the best displayed price.

### G2. Route ticker
Show concise stops/themes: sportsbook check, floor walk, food stop, next pulse. Do not fill it with fake real-world claims.

### G3. Parking stub
Use for a logistics joke or to mark a stop that was not worth making.

### G4. Restaurant check
Use for food/service continuity and the Dinner card.

### G5. Handwritten margin note
One Lou sentence that cuts through the report: “price gone,” “need the lineup,” “wrong shop,” “keep walking.”

### G6. Last Stop receipt
End with the actual action state: number of BET/LEAN/WAIT/PASS and risk, then Lou’s next destination or meal note.

## Report-to-scene routing

### BET
Use A5 plus at most one supporting route/food/floor module. The exact ticket is the centerpiece. Do not bury a real bet under story.

### LEAN
Use A4, A2 or B1. Lou stays close to the counter but keeps his wallet closed.

### WAIT
Use B1/B3, A4, C4, D3 or a logistics module. State the exact missing condition.

### PASS
Use B2, A3, C6 or F5. Lou discards the bad transaction and moves on.

### PRICE WORSENED / STALE
A3 + B2 is the natural pair: number moved before Lou arrived, coupon expires.

### BIG BOOK DISAGREEMENT
A2. Lou shops harder; he does not automatically bet.

### LINEUP / PARTICIPATION GATE
A6, C4 or D3. The waiting time can produce a floor/coffee scene, but the missing official information stays explicit.

### NO BET / $0 RISK
C2/C6, D1/D2/D4, or F5. Preserving bankroll and moving on is the correct story ending.

## Time-of-day routing

### Morning
Prefer A1 + D1/D3 + one coupon or floor module.

### Final morning
Prefer A2/A4 + B1/B3; food may shift from breakfast toward an early slice only if it fits continuity.

### 15:15 / afternoon
Prefer route movement, Two-slice lunch, parking/logistics and one Floor Walk module. Avoid repeating morning coffee unless it is part of a deliberate running joke.

### 18:15 / late
Prefer dinner check, late floor lap, final sportsbook window and Last Stop. The issue should feel like the route is winding down.

---

## Rotation safeguards

- Do not open consecutive issues with the same meal or the same sportsbook-counter setup.
- Do not use progressive + video poker + comp + cocktail service in every issue. One floor idea is often enough.
- Do not force pizza into every issue; use Two Slice naturally.
- Do not invent specific real casino or restaurant facts for color.
- Do not invent staff quotes that contain sports information unavailable in the authoritative report.
- Do not make Lou perpetually angry. He is sociable; crankiness is aimed mostly at bad value and unnecessary hassle.
- Do not make Lou a compulsive gambler. Walking away is one of his defining actions.
- Do not let food or floor scenes delay the reader from seeing a real BET.
- Do not reuse current teams/prices as permanent scene-bank canon.

## Production boundary

This scene bank is first-party fictional production material. It can create story, setting, dialogue and route continuity. It cannot create or alter sports recommendations, prices, fair values, buying thresholds, risk, or claims about current real-world casino conditions.
