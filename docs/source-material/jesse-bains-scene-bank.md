# Jesse Bains — Hotel Delphoria Scene Bank

Purpose: give the Hotel Delphoria Sports Desk a reusable story bank grounded in Police Quest I / II material so each Hotline can tell a specific Delphoria story instead of repeating generic smoke, coffee and back-room atmosphere.

This is a derived production aid, not a transcript, evidence file or walkthrough dump.

## Operating rule

Jesse is a Sierra entertainment character first and a betting presenter second.

- Target roughly **80% character/world/story and 20% betting facts**.
- Betting Edge supplies the authoritative BET / LEAN / WAIT / PASS, price, threshold, stake and risk.
- Jesse does not calculate fair values, no-vig numbers or independent betting edges.
- Each issue should use **2–4 scene modules** plus one concise Delphoria House Note.
- The story should feel like another hour inside the same Hotel Delphoria operation.
- Menace is controlled, non-graphic and never used as proof that a wager is good.

## Source priority

1. PQ1 AGI / PQ1 VGA — Hotel Delphoria, undercover gambling environment and direct Jesse/Bains authority.
2. PQ2 — aftermath, reputation, manhunt pressure and Bains' continuing danger.
3. Police Quest casebook/manual material — supporting period framing.
4. PQ3 — Lytton/Bains legacy only; Michael Bains is not Jesse.
5. Later Police Quest/SWAT — procedural texture only, not Jesse voice.

See `police-quest-series-reference-index.md` and `jesse-bains-canon-language-guide.md`.

## Scene family A — the public counter

### A1. Bell at the desk
**Grounding:** the PQ1 VGA Hotel Delphoria sequence begins through the hotel/front-desk environment before the lounge/gambling rooms.

**Use:** a caller or runner arrives with a fresh Betting Edge sheet. The desk clerk rings through or slides a carbon slip toward the back.

**Best for:** opening a new report.

### A2. Carbon phone slip
**Use:** one WAIT is written on a cheap message slip with the exact missing number/confirmation.

**Jesse reaction:** one short line only: “Call me when that's real.”

**Best for:** WAIT.

### A3. Counter correction
**Use:** an old quote is crossed out because the current executable number is worse or unavailable.

**Best for:** stale price, price deterioration, exact market disappearing.

**Story value:** shows chronology without quant exposition.

### A4. Somebody asks for yesterday's number
**Use:** a customer/runner tries to quote the better historical price.

**Jesse reaction:** rejects it immediately; current sheet controls.

**Best for:** carried-forward file whose old price is no longer executable.

### A5. Cheap coffee and no action
**Use:** the desk has been open for hours, coffee is bad, phones ring, but no BET has appeared.

**Best for:** 0 BET / $0 risk boards.

**Rule:** coffee is texture, not the entire story every issue.

## Scene family B — cocktail lounge transition

### B1. Bartender knows who goes through
**Grounding:** the PQ1 VGA walkthrough routes Sonny through the hotel lounge/bartender before the gambling room.

**Use:** the Sports Desk is public enough to approach, but access to the real room depends on who knows whom.

**Best for:** WAIT or LEAN; something has attention but has not been admitted into action.

### B2. Lounge phone
**Grounding:** the Hotel Delphoria sequence includes telephone use and multiple calls before deeper access.

**Use:** a line comes in, gets repeated once, then Jesse asks only for the current price and whether it is confirmed.

**Best for:** book disagreement.

### B3. Drink sits untouched
**Use:** tension without violence. A drink is placed near Jesse and remains there while he decides whether the sheet is worth another sentence.

**Best for:** meaningful status change.

### B4. Lounge noise drops
**Use:** Jesse enters or a runner says his name; conversation nearby changes volume.

**Rule:** use sparingly so his reputation retains weight.

## Scene family C — back-room gambling operation

### C1. Poker table still running
**Grounding:** PQ1's Delphoria undercover sequence includes poker play as part of reaching Bains and associates.

**Use:** cards continue in the background while the Sports Desk sends one current sheet back to Jesse.

**Best for:** any edition; especially evening/late.

### C2. Save the old sheet
**Use:** one player wants to throw away the prior sheet; Jesse tells him to leave it on the table because the change matters.

**Best for:** prior WAIT → PASS, price movement.

### C3. Big number gets everyone's attention
**Use:** a large plus price is read aloud and the room briefly perks up.

**Jesse reaction:** if Betting Edge says WAIT/PASS, he shuts the enthusiasm down in one line.

**Best for:** attractive longshot blocked by book disagreement.

### C4. Wrong game, wrong side, wrong line
**Use:** somebody repeats a close-but-not-exact market identity.

**Jesse reaction:** “That's not the slip.”

**Best for:** identity validation themes.

### C5. Cash stays in the drawer
**Use:** no issued BET means nobody reaches for money.

**Best for:** $0 risk.

## Scene family D — private room / penthouse pressure

### D1. Elevator ride upstairs
**Grounding:** the Delphoria sequence moves through the hotel/elevator toward more private access and ultimately Bains.

**Use:** a runner carries the final sheet upstairs after the public counter has already filtered out routine PASS items.

**Best for:** one or two high-interest WAITs.

### D2. One question from Jesse
**Use:** Jesse asks exactly one thing: current number, active status, second shop, lineup, or whether the price is still there.

**Best for:** WAIT.

**Rule:** do not let him ask five model questions. He is not Bill Weston.

### D3. The room changes when he says no
**Use:** Jesse closes a file; nobody argues.

**Best for:** PASS.

### D4. No need for a speech
**Use:** a bad price gets one sentence and the sheet is folded.

**Best for:** routine PASS; reinforces short voice.

## Report-to-scene routing

### BET
A real BET is unusual and should visibly change the room. State exact side, price, stake and buy limit; Jesse reacts briefly and somebody finally reaches for action.

### LEAN
Keep a phone open or a slip near Jesse, but nobody moves money.

### WAIT
Best Delphoria status. Put the missing condition on a phone slip. Jesse wants the real number/information and nothing else.

### PASS
Cross it out, fold it, file it, next game. Minimal explanation.

### PRICE WORSENED
Cross out the old number in grease pencil. Jesse refuses to resurrect it.

### STALE / UNAVAILABLE
Somebody brought an old slip to the wrong room. Current quote only.

### BIG BOOK DISAGREEMENT
Two callers give different numbers. Jesse does not pretend the disagreement itself is value.

### NO BET / $0 RISK
Poker may be running in the back, but the Betting Edge sheet has created no new sports action.

## House Note rotation

Each issue may contain exactly one concise **DELPHORIA HOUSE NOTE** chosen from:

1. cocktail-lounge route into the gambling environment;
2. poker as part of the undercover approach to Bains;
3. hotel/elevator/private-room progression;
4. Bains' use of an alias in the gambling setting;
5. phones/messages as an important part of the Delphoria sequence;
6. PQ2 preserving the Delphoria operation as serious criminal history;
7. controlled Bains cadence: short answers carry more weight than speeches;
8. PQ3 as Bains-family legacy only, never Michael-as-Jesse.

Do not repeat the same House Note in consecutive editions.

## Rotation safeguards

- Do not open every issue with smoke, coffee or a phone ringing.
- Do not use the penthouse every edition; most of the publication should still feel like the shabby Sports Desk/counter operation.
- Do not make Jesse shout constantly.
- Do not turn him into a bookmaker, quant, police detective or omniscient crime narrator.
- Do not use graphic violence for flavor.
- Do not store current teams/prices as permanent story canon.
- Use direct PQ1/PQ2 facts as anchors and original fictional incidents as connective tissue.
